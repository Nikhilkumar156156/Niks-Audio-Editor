How the AI Repeat Detector Works

Traditional audio repeat detectors use 1D volume curves, which lead to false positives (matching completely different words simply because their volume profiles look similar). The Niks Audio Editor implements a state-of-the-art 8-Band DFT Spectrogram Matching and 8D Subsequence DTW alignment pipeline:

1. 8-Band Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)

When you click "Scan for Repeats", the buffer is split into speech segments. For each segment, the editor computes short-time spectral frames at 20Hz. For each frame, we extract magnitudes at 8 log-spaced frequency bands (150Hz to 8kHz) using precomputed cosine/sine windowing tables. This extracts pitch, vowel formants, and sibilant frequencies, forming a unique frequency fingerprint.

2. Logarithmic Compression & Z-Score Band Normalization

Spectral magnitudes are logarithmically scaled: Y = log10(1 + 1000 * |X|) to align with human hearing dynamics. We then apply z-score normalization on each frequency band independently. This removes absolute volume differences, allowing a whisper and a shout of the same word to align perfectly.

3. 8-Dimensional Subsequence DTW (sDTW)

We align the two multi-dimensional spectrograms using Subsequence Dynamic Time Warping. The distance between frames is computed as the 8D Euclidean distance. Subsequence alignment matches the shorter query pattern inside the longer clip, detecting stumbles even if surrounded by breaths, background clicks, or extra speech.

4. Calibrated Decision Thresholds

To avoid false positives on common filler words (like "the" or "and"), candidates are filtered using strict rules: the segment must be at least 1.0 second long, the shorter candidate must be at least 45% of the longer candidate's duration, raw RMS energy must exceed 0.015, and the volume dynamics must match by at least 40%.

Timeline Color Codes

The timeline draws clips using distinctive glassmorphism colors with soft neon glow outlines:

  • Blue Segment: Normal speech segment. Drag, trim, fade, or speed it up.
  • Red Segment: Automatically scanned silent gap. Double-click to open actions, or click "Delete All Gaps" to clear them.
  • Yellow Segment: Detected speech stumble/repeat. These are the redundant takes that should be trimmed or deleted.
  • Green Segment: The final, clean speech take. This is the keeper track that remains on the timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is my audio sent to a server?

A: No. All audio analysis, speech segmentation, gap removal, spectral DTW alignment, and exporting (WAV, MP3, AAC) are done 100% locally in your browser. Your data never leaves your device.

Q: Why are some segments red?

A: Red segments represent silent gaps detected by the silence scanner. You can remove them individually or click "Delete All Gaps" to perform a ripple delete, which pulls all subsequent audio clips back to maintain a continuous, gap-free speech track.

Q: How do I export in MP3 or AAC format?

A: Choose your desired format (WAV, MP3, or AAC) from the dropdown selector in the right-hand footer toolbar, then click the "Export" button. The MP3 files are encoded using lamejs, and AAC files use the native WebCodecs API.

Q: Can I edit on my Android or tablet?

A: Yes! The editor features a responsive bottom settings drawer, touch-drag zoom and scroll gestures, larger range sliders, and root-level dialog positioning, optimized for mobile screens.
